Giethoorn: The Dutch Village with No Roads

WanderAmsterdam editorial · Published 2026-10-22

In the province of Overijssel, roughly two hours from Amsterdam, the old part of Giethoorn has no roads. Access to its farmhouses and cottages is by canal boat, on foot across one of its many small bridges, or by bicycle along narrow paths running beside the water. The village has been like this since it was founded around 1230 — the canals are the original "roads," dug over centuries as residents extracted peat from the marshy ground.

The name comes from the Dutch "gietehorns" (goat horns) — the horns of wild goats found buried in the peat by the village's first settlers. What those settlers accidentally created, by digging and flooding the peat bogs, was one of the most quietly beautiful villages in the Netherlands.

How the village works

The old village (Dorpsgracht area) covers about 6 kilometers of canals connected by around 50 traditional wooden footbridges. Thatched-roof farmhouses sit directly on the canal banks, accessible only by those bridges or by boat. Each house typically has its own small dock.

The characteristic vessels of Giethoorn are "whisper boats" — small electric motorboats that make almost no noise on the water. You can hire one without a license and navigate the canals yourself. The silence is genuinely striking: no engines, no traffic, just birdsong, water, and the occasional creak of a wooden bridge.

Self-drive boats: No boating license is required to hire a whisper boat in Giethoorn. Rental companies provide brief instructions and a map. A two-hour self-drive rental typically covers the main canal loop through the old village comfortably. Book ahead in summer; demand is high.

The broader Weerribben-Wieden area

Giethoorn sits within the De Weerribben-Wieden National Park, a vast wetland of peat bogs, reed marshes, and shallow lakes. The park is one of the largest areas of low-fen mire in northwestern Europe and supports rare species including the bittern, purple heron, and otters. Canoe and kayak routes extend far beyond the village itself, into quieter water systems where few tourists venture.

Getting there from Amsterdam

Giethoorn is not on a main rail line. The most practical approach from Amsterdam is by tour bus or organised day trip, which typically takes around two hours each way. Alternatively, take a train to Steenwijk (about 75 minutes) and then a bus or taxi for the final stretch. Driving is the most flexible option — the surrounding car parks feed into the village on foot.

Guided canal tours from Amsterdam's local tour operators run seasonal day trips to Giethoorn that handle the logistics and include a guided boat tour through the old canals.

When to visit

Summer (June–August) is peak season, and the village can feel crowded around midday. Early mornings and shoulder season visits (April, May, September) offer the village at its quietest and most photogenic. Winter visits are unusual but peaceful — frost over the canals and smoke from farmhouse chimneys create a different kind of atmosphere entirely.
