Amsterdam with Kids: Best Family Activities and Tips

WanderAmsterdam · Updated September 2026

Amsterdam is one of Europe's most family-friendly cities — flat streets, excellent public transport, short distances between attractions, and a culture that genuinely welcomes children. The trick is knowing which activities work for which ages and how to avoid the obvious tourist traps.

Here are the best things to do in Amsterdam with kids, organized by type, with honest age recommendations and practical tips.

Museums That Kids Actually Enjoy

NEMO Science Museum

The best kids' museum in Amsterdam, full stop. Five floors of hands-on experiments — chain reactions, bubble walls, water play, a mock chemistry lab, and a technology floor where teenagers build circuits. The green rooftop terrace has city views and a water play area in summer (free to access without a ticket). Plan 2 to 3 hours.

Ages 3-16 · Oosterdok · Closed Mondays (except school holidays)

Rijksmuseum

Not just for adults. The Rijksmuseum runs family tours and treasure hunts that keep kids engaged while you look at Rembrandt and Vermeer. Pick up the free family trail at the entrance — it sends kids hunting through the galleries for specific details in the paintings. The outdoor courtyard and gardens are free.

Ages 6+ · Museumplein · Free under 18

Tropenmuseum

An interactive museum about world cultures — kids walk through recreated marketplaces, try on costumes, and explore hands-on exhibits about music, food, and daily life in different countries. Tropenmuseum Junior runs dedicated workshops and immersive experiences for ages 6 to 13. Less crowded than the big three on Museumplein.

Ages 4-13 · Oost · Closed Mondays

National Maritime Museum

A full-size replica of a Dutch East India Company ship (the Amsterdam) sits in the harbor outside, and kids can climb aboard, explore the decks, and pretend to be 17th-century sailors. Inside, interactive exhibits cover navigation, trade, and sea life. The cannon demonstrations and knot-tying workshops are popular with ages 5 to 12.

Ages 4-12 · Kattenburgerplein · Ship access may have separate fee
Museum tip: The I amsterdam City Card covers entry to most museums and unlimited public transport. For families spending 2 or more days doing museums, it usually pays for itself. Kids under 18 get free entry at the Rijksmuseum, Van Gogh Museum, and several others regardless.

Outdoor Activities

Vondelpark

Amsterdam's biggest park has six playgrounds, a paddling pool (summer), a skate park, and open lawns for running around. The main playground near Het Groot Melkhuis cafe is the best — a large wooden play structure with swings and climbing frames. Grab coffee at the cafe while kids play. Free entry, always open.

All ages · Museum Quarter · Free

Family Bike Tour

Rent a bakfiets (cargo bike) with a box on the front for small children, or book a guided family bike tour that sticks to quiet streets and park paths. Kids ride in the cargo box while you pedal — they love it, and it covers more ground than walking. MacBike and Black Bikes rent cargo bikes from around 25 euros per day.

Ages 0-6 in cargo bike · City-wide · Helmet recommended for kids

Artis Royal Zoo

One of Europe's oldest zoos, with over 900 animal species plus a planetarium, aquarium, butterfly garden, and a microbe museum (Micropia — separate entry, better for ages 8 and up). The zoo is compact enough to cover in 3 to 4 hours. The aquarium and nocturnal animal house are highlights for young kids.

All ages · Plantage · Allow 3-4 hours

Amsterdam-Noord by Ferry

The free ferry from Central Station to Amsterdam-Noord is an adventure in itself for kids — a five-minute boat ride across the IJ river. On the other side, NDSM Wharf has street art, playgrounds, and open space. In summer, the Pllek beach bar has a sandy area where kids can dig while you sit on the waterfront.

All ages · Central Station ferry · Free

On the Water

Family Canal Cruise

A canal cruise is the single best activity to do with kids in Amsterdam. They get to sit on a boat, see the city from the water, spot bikes and houseboats, and go under bridges. Standard cruises last 60 to 75 minutes — just right before attention wanders. Open-air boats are best in summer; covered boats with heaters in winter. Browse our tours page for family-friendly options.

All ages · Canal ring · 60-75 minutes

Pannenkoekenboot (Pancake Boat)

A boat cruise where kids eat unlimited Dutch pancakes while floating through the harbor. It departs from the NDSM Wharf in Noord. The pancakes are simple but kids think it is the greatest thing that has ever happened to them. Book in advance — it sells out on weekends and school holidays.

Ages 2-12 · NDSM Wharf · 75 minutes · Book ahead

Pedal Boat on the Canals

Rent a pedal boat from one of the canal-side rental stations and let the family power their own boat through the canals. Kids old enough to help pedal will want a turn. Boats fit 2 to 4 people and cost about 15 to 20 euros per hour. No license or experience needed — you just pedal and steer.

Ages 4+ · Multiple rental points · From 15 euros/hour

Rainy Day Options

Kinderkookkafe

A cafe in Vondelpark where children prepare and serve their own food — with real knives, real stoves, and real ingredients. Kids aged 5 and up take over the kitchen under supervision and make simple dishes. It is messy, chaotic, and completely charming. One of Amsterdam's most original family experiences.

Ages 5-12 · Vondelpark · Check opening times

TunFun Indoor Playground

A large underground play space in a former traffic tunnel near Waterlooplein. Ball pits, trampolines, climbing walls, and a toddler section. Not the most elegant venue but kids burn enormous amounts of energy here, which is exactly what you need on a wet Amsterdam afternoon.

Ages 1-12 · Waterlooplein · Indoor

Practical Tips for Families

Getting around. Amsterdam's tram system is stroller-friendly — most trams have low floors. Kids under 4 ride free on public transport. Buy an OV-chipkaart or day pass for the family. Walking works for most of the center, but distances add up with small legs — a taxi back to the hotel at the end of the day is worth the 10 euros.

Watch the bike lanes. This is the single most important safety tip. Amsterdam's red-paved bike lanes are not pedestrian space. Cyclists move fast, don't always ring their bell, and have right of way. Hold young children's hands when crossing, and teach older kids to look left before stepping off the curb.

Dining with kids. Most Amsterdam restaurants welcome children, but kitchens open late by American standards — dinner service typically starts at 6 PM. Indonesian restaurants and pancake houses (pannenkoekenhuis) are reliably kid-friendly. Albert Heijn supermarkets sell ready-made sandwiches, fruit, and snacks if you need a quick picnic in the park.

Best neighborhoods for families. Museum Quarter (Museumplein) puts you near Vondelpark, the Rijksmuseum, and wide sidewalks. De Pijp has a village feel with Albert Cuyp Market. The Jordaan is charming but the narrow canal-side paths require extra vigilance with toddlers near the water.

When to visit. April through June is ideal — tulip season, mild weather, longer days, and lighter crowds than July and August. King's Day (April 27) is exciting but very crowded with young kids. December brings Sinterklaas festivities and ice skating on Museumplein — a festive time for families who don't mind cold weather.

Budget tip: Free activities add up fast in Amsterdam. Vondelpark, the ferry to Noord, the Rijksmuseum courtyard, the Bloemenmarkt walk, and the Albert Cuyp Market are all free. Save the paid attractions for NEMO, the zoo, and one canal cruise — that covers a full family trip without overspending.
