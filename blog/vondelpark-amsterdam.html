Vondelpark: Amsterdam's Beloved Green Heart

WanderAmsterdam editorial · Published 2026-10-22

Vondelpark is Amsterdam's largest and most visited city park — 47 hectares of English-style landscape garden in the middle of the city, drawing over 12 million visitors a year. On a warm summer Sunday it's packed with cyclists, inline skaters, families with picnics, musicians giving informal concerts, and dogs pursuing squirrels across the lawns. On a grey Tuesday morning in November it's nearly empty, with herons standing motionless in the ponds and the Amsterdam parakeets screeching through the plane trees.

The park was laid out on marshland in 1865 by landscape architect Jan David Zocher and expanded to its current size by his son, Louis Paul Zocher, by 1877. It was originally called Nieuwe Park; the current name was adopted in 1867 when a statue of the 17th-century Dutch poet Joost van den Vondel was installed at the entrance. Amsterdam declared it a national monument in 1996.

What draws 12 million people a year

The open-air theatre

The Openluchttheater in the middle of Vondelpark runs a free summer performance program from June through August — theatre, music, cabaret, and children's shows on an outdoor stage. No booking required; audiences bring blankets and food and sit on the grass. It is one of Amsterdam's most pleasantly egalitarian institutions.

Cycling through the park

Dedicated cycle paths cross the park in multiple directions. For visitors staying near Museumplein, Vondelpark is a natural cycling route between the museums and the Jordaan neighborhood — a greener and more pleasant alternative to the main roads. Bike rental shops cluster around both park entrances.

The café pavilions

Two historic pavilions within the park house full-service restaurants and cafés: Vondelpark3 and Blauwe Theehuis (Blue Teahouse). The Blauwe Theehuis — a circular modernist building from 1937 — is particularly well-known; its outdoor terrace wraps around the building and fills on sunny afternoons.

Rose garden: The formal rose garden in the eastern section of Vondelpark contains over 70 varieties. Late June is peak blooming season. The garden sits adjacent to one of the park's main ponds, making it among the most photographed corners.

Vondelpark and Amsterdam's bike culture

On warm weekends, the paths through Vondelpark become a cross-section of Amsterdam's cycling identity: cargo bikes carrying small children, road cyclists in lycra, elderly couples on upright city bikes, tourists wobbling on rented bikes, and teenagers on electric models. The park's wide, flat paths make it an ideal place to get comfortable on a bicycle before venturing into Amsterdam's main traffic.

Guided bike tours through Vondelpark and the surrounding Oud-Zuid and Jordaan neighborhoods are available through Amsterdam bike tour operators — a good way to orientate yourself in the city's western neighborhoods.

Location and access

Vondelpark sits between Museumplein and the Oud-West neighborhood. Tram lines 1, 2, 5, and 11 stop at Leidseplein, a short walk from the eastern entrance. The park is open 24 hours. Entry is free.
