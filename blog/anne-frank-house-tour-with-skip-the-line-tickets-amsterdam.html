Anne Frank House Tickets & Tours: Prices, Release Times and What to Do When It's Sold Out

WanderAmsterdam editorial · Published 2026-06-13 · Updated 26 October 2026

Anne Frank House tickets are sold one way only: online, from the museum itself, in timed slots that go on sale every Tuesday for dates six weeks out. Nobody else sells them. No tour includes them. Nothing is sold at the door. Once you know that, the rest of planning a visit is easy: this guide covers the current prices, the exact release schedule, how to actually get a slot, and the Anne Frank tours in Amsterdam that are worth booking whether or not you get inside.

The short version (checked October 2026): Adult €16.50 · ages 10–17 €7 · ages 0–9 €1 (every person needs a ticket). Released Tuesdays at 10:00 Amsterdam time for visits six weeks later, only at annefrank.org. Open daily 9:00–22:00. Not sold on site. Not resold by any tour company.

Prices are set by the museum and change rarely; if you see "Anne Frank House tickets" for €40–€80 anywhere else, you are looking at a walking tour that does not go inside (more on that below).

How to buy Anne Frank House tickets: the release schedule

The museum releases every slot for a single day at once, six weeks ahead, each Tuesday at 10:00 CET/CEST. Example: tickets for Saturday 12 December 2026 go on sale Tuesday 3 November at 10:00 Amsterdam time. In spring and summer the popular daytime slots are gone in minutes; evening slots (after about 18:30) and winter weekdays last longer, sometimes days.

  1. Count back six weeks from the date you want and find the Tuesday that falls on or just before it.
  2. At 09:55 Amsterdam time that Tuesday, open the official ticket page. There is a queue system; don't refresh.
  3. Pick your slot, enter every visitor (children too), pay. Tickets arrive by email as a QR code.
  4. Missed it? Re-check the same page over the following days. Returned and unsold slots reappear without announcement, most often for evenings.

There is no waiting list, no on-the-day queue and no reseller. The museum states plainly that tickets are not sold at the door and that no guided tour includes admission.

Anne Frank tours in Amsterdam: what they include (and don't)

Because the museum keeps every ticket for itself, every "Anne Frank House tour" sold by a tour company is a walking tour of the neighbourhood and the Jewish Quarter, ending at or passing the house's exterior. Sold out? What to do instead

If your dates have no slots left, these are the honest alternatives, roughly in order of how close they get you to the story:

  • Keep checking the official page — cancellations are re-released, evenings most often. Try early morning and late evening Amsterdam time.
  • Walk the route with a guide. The tours below follow the family's last walk to Prinsengracht 263, stand under the Westerkerk bells Anne wrote about, and cover the deportations from the Jewish Quarter. One includes a virtual-reality walk-through of the annex rooms, which is the closest you can get without a ticket.
  • Jewish Quarter museums. The National Holocaust Museum (opened 2024), the Jewish Museum and the Portuguese Synagogue are a short tram ride east and far easier to enter.
  • The Resistance Museum (Verzetsmuseum) opposite Artis zoo covers the occupation years with personal stories and rarely needs advance booking.

What you see inside, and how long it takes

The museum is Prinsengracht 263, the canal house where Otto Frank's pectin and spice business ran downstairs while eight people hid in the rear annex from July 1942 to August 1944. You move one way: the warehouse and offices where the helpers worked, the bookcase that hid the staircase, then the annex rooms themselves. Otto Frank asked that they stay unfurnished after the war, so you walk through empty rooms with Anne's magazine cuttings still on the wall of hers. The route ends with the diary and her later notebooks.

Plan 60–90 minutes inside. The audio guide is included and paces you through. Add 30 minutes if you booked the introductory programme. Arrive within your 30-minute entry window; there's no early entry and no grace period after it.

Practical details

Opening hours

Daily 9:00–22:00. Shorter hours on some public holidays; closed on Yom Kippur (21 September 2026). Last slots start around 21:00.

Best time to visit

The first slots (9:00–10:00) and anything after 18:30 are quietest. Those evening slots are also the ones most likely to still be available on release day, and the most common to reappear after cancellations. Winter weekdays are the easiest tickets of the year.

Accessibility

The museum says it directly: steep stairs and a high step. The annex itself cannot be reached by lift, wheelchair or stroller. The modern museum wing and a video presentation of the annex are accessible; check the museum's accessibility page before booking if stairs are a problem.

With children

Every child needs their own €1 or €7 ticket. The museum suggests the visit for ages 10 and up; younger children can enter but the content is unsoftened. The walking tours above are generally pitched at 12+.

Photos and bags

No photography anywhere inside. Small bags only; large bags and backpacks must be checked.

What to combine it with

  • Westerkerk, next door — the bell tower Anne could see from the attic. Climbable in season.
  • The Jordaan, the neighbourhood behind the house: cafés, the Noordermarkt on Saturdays, and our Jordaan guide.
  • A canal cruise from the museum's doorstep — two operators board beside it (cards above); see which canal cruise to pick.
  • Rijksmuseum or Van Gogh later the same day, a deliberate change of register; both also need timed tickets, see our museum guide.

Anne Frank House tickets: questions people ask

Can you buy Anne Frank House tickets at the door?

No. The museum sells no tickets on site and there is no walk-in line. Every ticket is sold online at annefrank.org for a timed slot.

How far in advance do Anne Frank House tickets go on sale?

Six weeks ahead. All slots for a given day are released on a Tuesday at 10:00 Amsterdam time, six weeks before the visit date. Unsold and returned slots can reappear later.

How much are Anne Frank House tickets in 2026?

€16.50 for adults, €7 for ages 10–17, €1 for ages 0–9, including the €1 booking fee. Museumkaart and ICOM holders pay €1 but must still reserve a slot.

Does any guided tour include Anne Frank House entry?

No. The museum does not sell tickets to tour companies or resellers. "Anne Frank tours" are walking tours of the neighbourhood and Jewish Quarter that end at or pass the house's exterior; a few include a virtual-reality view of the annex.

Are Anne Frank House tours worth it if you can't get inside?

Yes for most visitors. A good guide covers the family's route, the helpers, the Westerkerk and the deportations from the Jewish Quarter, which is the context the empty rooms assume you already know. Many people do the walk the day before their museum slot.

How long does the Anne Frank House take?

Allow 60–90 minutes inside, plus 30 minutes if you add the introductory programme. Walking tours run 2–2.5 hours.

What are the Anne Frank House opening hours?

Daily 9:00 to 22:00, with reduced hours on a few public holidays and closure on Yom Kippur. Check the official site for the exact day you're visiting.

What if Anne Frank House tickets are sold out for my dates?

Re-check the official page daily, especially mornings and late evenings Amsterdam time, for re-released slots. Book a walking tour with the VR annex as the fallback, and add the Holocaust Museum or Resistance Museum, which rarely sell out.
