Van Gogh Museum Amsterdam: The Complete Visitor's Guide

WanderAmsterdam editorial · Published 2026-10-22

Amsterdam's Van Gogh Museum holds the world's largest collection of works by Vincent van Gogh: more than 200 paintings, 500 drawings, and around 700 personal letters. The collection spans his entire career — from the dark, earthy palette of his Dutch period through the explosive color of his years in Arles and the visionary late works painted in Saint-Rémy and Auvers-sur-Oise. Seeing it in sequence over two or three hours gives a sense of his development that no other museum in the world can match.

The museum opened in 1973 on Museumplein, in a building designed by Gerrit Rietveld. It draws over 1.8 million visitors per year, making it one of the busiest museums in Europe.

What to see: the five periods of Van Gogh's work

The permanent collection is arranged chronologically, tracing Van Gogh's development through five geographic periods.

The Netherlands (1880–1886)

Van Gogh's early Dutch work is characterized by dark tones, thick impasto, and subjects drawn from peasant life. The Potato Eaters (1885) — a family gathered around a lamp, eating potatoes in near-darkness — is the major work of this period and one of the museum's most discussed paintings. The color is deliberately somber; Van Gogh wanted to convey physical labor through the darkness of the palette itself.

Paris (1886–1888)

Moving to Paris transformed Van Gogh's palette. Exposure to Impressionism, Pointillism, and Japanese woodblock prints lightened and brightened his work dramatically. The self-portraits from this period — the museum holds several — show the experimentation of an artist absorbing new influences.

Arles (1888–1889)

In Arles, working in intense Provençal light, Van Gogh produced some of his best-known images. Bedroom in Arles, multiple versions of Sunflowers, and The Yellow House all date from this period. The letters to his brother Theo from Arles — hundreds of them — are among the most vivid artistic correspondence in Western history.

The letters: The museum displays selections of Van Gogh's letters to Theo alongside the works they describe. Reading a letter and then standing in front of the painting it discusses is one of the distinctive experiences the collection makes possible.

Saint-Rémy (1889–1890) and Auvers (1890)

The final two periods produced the most turbulent and visionary work. Almond Blossom (1890), painted for Theo's newborn son, radiates calm. Wheatfield with Crows, often described as one of Van Gogh's last paintings, fills the upper galleries with its heavy sky. Van Gogh died in July 1890, aged 37.

Practical visitor information

Tickets and reservations

Timed-entry tickets must be booked in advance at vangoghmuseum.nl. The museum does not sell tickets at the door. Weekend and peak-season dates sell out weeks ahead. Book as far in advance as possible.

Best times to visit

Opening time (9:00 AM) and the final two hours before closing are the least crowded. Friday evenings have extended hours during some seasons. Weekday mornings in autumn and winter offer the most space around popular works.

The building and garden

The main building by Rietveld connects via a passageway to a 1999 exhibition wing designed by Kisho Kurokawa. Between them is a small garden accessible from the ground floor.
