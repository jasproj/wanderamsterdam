3 Days in Amsterdam: The Perfect Itinerary

WanderAmsterdam editorial · Published 2026-08-30

Three days in Amsterdam is enough to see the best museums, cruise the canals, eat your way through two neighborhoods, and still have a morning for a windmill day trip. The city is compact — most of what you'll want to see is within a 30-minute walk of Centraal Station. This itinerary keeps things tight without feeling rushed.

Before you go: Book the Rijksmuseum, Van Gogh Museum, and Anne Frank House online well in advance. Anne Frank House tickets sell out weeks ahead. Buy an OV-chipkaart at the airport or station for tram and metro rides.

Day 1: The Canal Ring, Jordaan, and First Impressions

Orient yourself on the water, then explore Amsterdam's most charming neighborhood

Morning: Canal Cruise

Start with a canal cruise — it's the fastest way to understand Amsterdam's layout. A 60–75 minute cruise covers the main canals, passes under bridges, and points out landmarks you'll visit later. Book an open-top boat if the weather is good. Depart from near Centraal Station; most operators run boats every 15–30 minutes.

60–75 min · From ~€15

Late Morning: The Jordaan

Walk west from the canal ring into the Jordaan. Wander the narrow streets without a plan — Bloemgracht, Egelantiersgracht, and the Karthuizerhof (a hidden courtyard off Karthuizersstraat). Stop at Winkel 43 for appeltaart (Dutch apple pie), widely considered the best in Amsterdam, or find a terrace café on one of the small canals.

Free to explore

Afternoon: Anne Frank House

Your pre-booked timed slot at the Anne Frank House on Prinsengracht. Allow 60–90 minutes inside. Afterward, walk south along Prinsengracht to the Nine Streets (De Negen Straatjes) for boutique shopping and independent cafés.

~€16 · 60–90 min

Evening: Brown Café and Bitterballen

End the day in a brown café — Café 't Smalle (1786, on Egelantiersgracht) is perfect. Order bitterballen with mustard and a local beer. If you want dinner, walk to De Pijp for Indonesian rijsttafel at Blauw or Surinamese roti at Roopram Roti.

Bitterballen ~€8 · Beer ~€5

Day 2: Museumplein and De Pijp

World-class art in the morning, Amsterdam's best food neighborhood in the afternoon

Morning: Rijksmuseum

Book the 9am slot. Head straight for the Gallery of Honour and Rembrandt's Night Watch before the crowds build. Then work through the Vermeer rooms, the Delft pottery, and the Golden Age galleries. The Cuypers Library on the first floor is one of the most beautiful rooms in Amsterdam. Allow 2–3 hours minimum.

~€22.50 · 9am slot recommended

Late Morning: Van Gogh Museum

A five-minute walk across Museumplein. The chronological layout means you follow Van Gogh's artistic development from the dark Dutch period to the explosive color of his final years. Sunflowers, Almond Blossom, The Bedroom, and his letters to Theo are all here. Allow 1.5–2 hours.

~€20 · Next door to Rijksmuseum

Afternoon: Albert Cuyp Market and De Pijp

Walk or tram south to De Pijp. Lunch at Albert Cuyp Market — fresh stroopwafels, herring, Surinamese roti, and cheese samples as you walk the length of the market. Then explore the side streets of De Pijp: Sarphatipark for a sit-down, the Heineken Experience if that's your thing, and the multicultural restaurant strip on Eerste van der Helststraat.

Market Mon–Sat 9am–5pm

Evening: Dinner in De Pijp

De Pijp has the best concentration of restaurants in Amsterdam. CT Coffee and Coconuts (in a former cinema), Bar Fisk (Mediterranean seafood), or Firma Pekelhaaring (Dutch-French) for dinner. Or head to Foodhallen in Oud-West for a curated indoor food hall with 20+ vendors.

Budget €20–40 per person

Day 3: Day Trip or Amsterdam Noord

Choose your own adventure — windmills or the creative side of the city

Option A: Zaanse Schans Day Trip

Take the train from Centraal Station to Zaandijk-Zaanse Schans (17 minutes). Spend the morning walking among working windmills, watching cheese and clog making, and photographing the green wooden houses along the Zaan River. Back in Amsterdam by lunchtime. More day trip ideas →

17 min by train · Free entry to site

Option B: Amsterdam Noord

Take the free ferry from behind Centraal Station (5 minutes) to Noord. Visit the NDSM Wharf for street art and weekend markets, the EYE Film Museum for architecture and exhibitions, and A'DAM Lookout for the rooftop swing 100 meters above the city. Pllek (beach bar on the waterfront) is the best lunch spot in Noord.

Free ferry · 5 min from Centraal

Afternoon: Vondelpark and Final Walks

Back in the centre, spend the afternoon in Vondelpark — bring a coffee from a nearby café and sit on the grass. Walk Brouwersgracht (Amsterdam's prettiest canal) as the afternoon light hits the water. Do any last shopping in the Nine Streets. Pick up aged Gouda from De Kaaskamer to take home.

Free

Evening: Final Dinner

Splurge on a farewell dinner — Restaurant Breda (Southern French-Dutch), Rijks (modern Dutch at the Rijksmuseum), or a rijsttafel at Kantjil & de Tijger. Or keep it simple: a cheese board and jenever at a canal-side terrace as the sun goes down.

Have more time? Add a kayak tour on day 4, visit the Stedelijk Museum, explore Haarlem by train (15 minutes), or spend a morning at the Waterlooplein flea market and the Jewish Historical Museum.
