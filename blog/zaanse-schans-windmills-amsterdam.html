Zaanse Schans: Amsterdam's Working Windmill Village

WanderAmsterdam editorial · Published 2026-10-22

Just fifteen minutes north of Amsterdam by train, the Zaanse Schans preserves what the Dutch landscape looked like three centuries ago. Five or six working windmills stand along the river Zaan — their sails turning in the wind when conditions allow — alongside green timber houses, craft workshops, and small museums where cheese is still made by hand and wooden clogs are carved from alder trunks.

This is not a theme park built for tourists. The windmills are working machines and the craftspeople are practicing actual trades. The Zaanse Schans was assembled from the 1960s onwards by relocating historic buildings from across the Zaan region to rescue them from demolition, creating an open-air village that draws around 2.6 million visitors a year.

The windmills

Three hundred years ago the banks of the river Zaan were one of the first industrial landscapes in the world, with several hundred windmills sawing timber for Dutch shipyards, pressing oil, grinding pigments, spices, and cocoa. Peter the Great visited in 1697 to study Dutch shipbuilding technology — lessons that shaped Russian industry for generations.

The five or six mills you'll see today are each dedicated to a specific process:

  • De Gekroonde Poelenburg — saws hardwood timber
  • De Kat — grinds pigments and dyes; the only working paint mill in the world
  • De Zoeker — presses oil from seeds
  • De Bonte Hen and Het Pink — grind spices

Entering the mills individually costs around €7.50 each. From inside you can watch the wooden gears and millstones in motion and climb to the cap for views over the river.

Wind matters: The mills only operate when there is enough wind. A calm day means stationary sails, which is still visually striking — but if you want to see them turning, check the weather forecast. Breezy weekday mornings in autumn and spring are typically the best combination of conditions and fewer crowds.

What else to see

Cheese workshop

The cheese farm demonstrates the traditional process of making Gouda cheese from raw milk, from curdling through pressing and brining. Tastings are included. The Dutch cheese market culture that made towns like Gouda and Alkmaar famous has its roots in this kind of artisan production.

Clog workshop

A craftsman carves klompen (wooden clogs) from single alder blocks using a machine originally designed in the 1800s. The Dutch wore wooden clogs for centuries as waterproof, durable work footwear. The workshop explains the process and sells both traditional and decorative versions.

Houses and history museums

Several of the green timber houses are open as small museums. The Zaans Museum provides broader context about the region's industrial history and the Monet connection — the painter visited Zaandam in 1871 and produced around 25 canvases of the village and its surroundings.

Getting there and practical tips

Direct trains from Amsterdam Centraal take around 17 minutes to Zaandijk-Zaanse Schans station, from which the village is a 15-minute walk. Entry to the Zaanse Schans grounds is free; individual attractions charge their own admission. Allow 3–4 hours for a thorough visit.
