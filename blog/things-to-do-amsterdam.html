20 Best Things to Do in Amsterdam

WanderAmsterdam editorial · Published 2026-08-30

Amsterdam is a city that looks effortless and isn't. Those canal houses lean at precise angles. The bike lanes run on infrastructure most cities haven't built. The museums are world-class because the Dutch spent centuries buying and commissioning art with trade money. It's a compact, walkable city where the best experiences are often free — sitting by a canal, getting lost in the Jordaan, watching boats from a bridge at sunset.

Here are 20 things worth your time, from the museums everyone knows to the neighborhoods most visitors skip.

1. Rijksmuseum

The national museum — and Rembrandt's Night Watch

The Rijksmuseum holds 8,000 objects across 80 galleries spanning 800 years of Dutch art and history. The Gallery of Honour — the long hall leading to Rembrandt's Night Watch — is one of the great museum experiences in Europe. Vermeer's Milkmaid, Delft pottery, Golden Age still lifes, and the 17th-century dollhouses are all here. The building itself, by Pierre Cuypers, is a work of art.

~€22.50 · Allow 2–3 hours
Tip: Book a timed-entry slot online — walk-up queues can run an hour in summer. Early morning (9am) and late afternoon (after 3pm) are the least crowded times.

2. Canal Cruise

See Amsterdam from the water

The canal ring is a UNESCO World Heritage site, and a canal cruise is the best way to understand the city's layout. Standard cruises run 60–75 minutes and cover the Herengracht, Prinsengracht, and Amstel. Open-top boats are best in good weather; covered boats run year-round. Cheese and wine cruises add Dutch food to the experience.

From ~€15 · 60–75 min

3. Anne Frank House

The Secret Annex where Anne Frank hid for two years

The house on Prinsengracht 263 where Anne Frank, her family, and four others hid from 1942 to 1944 is now a museum. Walking through the bookcase entrance into the annex rooms — seeing the photos Anne pasted on her wall, the pencil marks tracking the children's growth — is one of the most powerful museum experiences in Europe. The audio guide lets Anne's diary narrate as you move through the rooms.

~€16 · Book 6 weeks ahead
Tip: Tickets release online 6 weeks before the visit date at 10am CET. Set a calendar reminder — they sell out within hours. No walk-up availability.

4. Van Gogh Museum

The world's largest Van Gogh collection

Over 200 paintings, 500 drawings, and 700 letters trace Van Gogh's development from dark Dutch realism through his explosion of color in Arles and Saint-Rémy. Sunflowers, The Bedroom, Almond Blossom, and Wheatfield with Crows are all here. The chronological layout makes his artistic evolution tangible — you watch him become Van Gogh in real time.

~€20 · Allow 1.5–2 hours

5. Jordaan Neighborhood

The neighborhood that defines Amsterdam's charm

Just west of the canal ring, the Jordaan is narrow streets, hidden courtyards (hofjes), independent shops, and brown cafés. It was working-class until the 1970s; now it's one of the most desirable neighborhoods in the city. The Saturday Noordermarkt (organic produce, vintage) and Monday Noordermarkt (textiles, antiques) are both worth the trip. Wander without a plan — the Jordaan rewards getting lost.

Free to explore

6. Vondelpark

Amsterdam's Central Park

47 hectares of lawns, ponds, and paths in the middle of the city — perfect for a break between museums (the Rijksmuseum and Van Gogh Museum are on its northeast edge). Locals picnic, jog, and sit in the open-air Blauwe Theehuis café. The Groot Melkhuis has a playground. On warm weekends the park fills up but never feels overcrowded — it's designed to absorb people.

Free · Open 24 hours

7. Albert Cuyp Market

The biggest street market in Amsterdam

Over 250 stalls stretch along Albert Cuypstraat in De Pijp, six days a week (closed Sundays). Fresh stroopwafels, herring, Dutch cheese, flowers, clothing, and household goods. It's a working market, not a tourist market — the prices are fair and the stroopwafels are made fresh on the spot. The surrounding streets in De Pijp are worth exploring for their restaurants and cafés.

Mon–Sat 9am–5pm · Free to walk

8. Bike Through the City

Do as the Dutch do

Amsterdam has more bikes than people. Renting one gives you the freedom to cover the city like a local — the bike lanes are excellent, and distances between neighborhoods shrink to minutes. MacBike and Black Bikes rent from multiple locations. Stick to the bike lanes, signal your turns, and don't stop in the lane — local cyclists will let you know if you're in the way.

Rental from ~€12/day
Tip: If you're not confident cycling in traffic, book a guided bike tour first — it teaches you the rules and the routes before you go solo.

9. De Pijp Neighborhood

Amsterdam's most multicultural quarter

South of the canal ring, De Pijp has the Albert Cuyp Market, the Heineken Experience, Sarphatipark, and the best concentration of international restaurants in the city. Surinamese, Turkish, Moroccan, and Indonesian food sit alongside trendy brunch spots and craft cocktail bars. It's where young Amsterdammers go out, and it feels noticeably different from the canal ring tourist zone.

Tram 3, 4, or 12

10. Heineken Experience

Interactive brewery tour in the original building

The original Heineken brewery in De Pijp closed in 1988 and reopened as an interactive tour. It covers the brewing process, the brand's history, and ends with beer tastings and a rooftop bar. The self-guided tour takes about 90 minutes. It's polished and commercial but well done — and the two beers included in the ticket price soften any skepticism.

~€23 · Allow 90 min

11. Amsterdam Noord

The creative side of the IJ

A free ferry ride from Centraal Station drops you in Noord — a former shipyard district that's become Amsterdam's creative hub. The NDSM Wharf has street art, studios, and weekend markets. A'DAM Lookout tower has a rooftop swing 100 meters above the city. The EYE Film Museum is architecturally stunning even if you don't go in. Noord feels like a different city — raw, spacious, and still evolving.

Free ferry from Centraal · 5 min

12. Nine Streets (De Negen Straatjes)

Boutique shopping in the canal ring

Nine cross-streets connecting the main canals between Raadhuisstraat and Leidsestraat, packed with independent boutiques, vintage shops, cafés, and galleries. It's the best shopping area in Amsterdam for anything that isn't a chain. Vintage clothing, Dutch design, specialty cheese shops, and some of the city's best brunch spots are all concentrated in a walkable grid.

Free to walk

13. Stedelijk Museum

Modern and contemporary art

The third museum on Museumplein (alongside the Rijksmuseum and Van Gogh), the Stedelijk holds works by Mondrian, Malevich, Warhol, de Kooning, and Yayoi Kusama. The collection starts with the Impressionists and runs to the present. The "bathtub" extension by Benthem Crouwel is love-it-or-hate-it architecture. Less crowded than its neighbors, and often the best temporary exhibitions in the city.

~€22.50 · Allow 1.5 hours

14. Kayak the Canals

Paddle through the canal ring at water level

A guided kayak tour puts you on the water at eye level with the houseboats and canal-house foundations. Routes typically cover quieter canals away from the tour-boat traffic — Brouwersgracht, Bloemgracht, and the Jordaan waterways. No experience needed; the guides teach you in 10 minutes. It's the most intimate way to see Amsterdam's waterways.

From ~€35 · 2 hours

15. Begijnhof

A hidden medieval courtyard in the city center

Through an unmarked door off Spui square, the Begijnhof is a peaceful enclosed courtyard of 14th-century houses surrounding a garden and two chapels. It was originally home to a lay Catholic sisterhood (the Beguines). The oldest wooden house in Amsterdam (circa 1420) is here. Visitors are welcome but it's a residential space — the signs asking for quiet are serious.

Free · Open daily until sunset

16. Brouwersgracht

Amsterdam's most beautiful canal

Ask an Amsterdammer which canal is the prettiest and most will say Brouwersgracht. The former brewery canal at the top of the Jordaan has wider water, bigger houseboats, and a view west that catches the sunset. Walk it from Centraal Station toward the Jordaan, or grab a table at a canalside café. Less busy than Prinsengracht, more photogenic than Herengracht.

Free

17. FOAM Photography Museum

World-class photography in a canal house

FOAM hosts rotating exhibitions in a canal house on Keizersgracht — the spaces are intimate, the curation is sharp, and the exhibitions change frequently enough that repeat visits are worthwhile. Past shows have covered Viviane Sassen, Ai Weiwei, Daido Moriyama, and Anton Corbijn. It's small enough to see in an hour and always worth checking what's on.

~€14 · Allow 1 hour

18. Dutch Food Tour

Bitterballen, herring, stroopwafels, and jenever

Dutch food is better than its reputation. Guided food tours through the Jordaan or De Pijp cover bitterballen (fried meat ragout), raw herring with onions, aged Gouda, poffertjes (mini pancakes), stroopwafels, and jenever (Dutch gin). Eating Amsterdam and Hungry Birds run well-reviewed tours. They teach you what to order in a brown café and where locals actually eat.

From ~€80 · 3–4 hours

19. Day Trip to Zaanse Schans

Windmills, clogs, and cheese — 20 minutes from the city

A cluster of preserved windmills and workshops on the Zaan River, Zaanse Schans shows what the Dutch countryside looked like in the 18th century. You can enter working windmills, watch cheese and clog making, and walk along the river. It's touristy but the windmills are real and functional. Take the train to Zaandijk-Zaanse Schans (17 minutes from Centraal) rather than paying for an organized tour. More day trips by train →

Free entry · 17 min by train

20. Brown Cafés

The Dutch pub tradition

Brown cafés (bruine kroegen) are Amsterdam's version of the local pub — dark wood, stained walls (from centuries of tobacco smoke, now banned), beer on tap, and a reading table with newspapers. Café 't Smalle in the Jordaan, Café Chris (the oldest brown café, since 1624), and Café de Vergulde Gaper on Prinseneiland are three of the best. Order a biertje (beer) or a kopstoot (beer with a jenever chaser) and stay a while.

Beer from ~€5
