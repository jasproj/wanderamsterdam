Keukenhof & the Dutch Tulip Fields: A Spring Pilgrimage Guide

WanderAmsterdam editorial · Published 2026-10-22

Every spring, for roughly eight weeks, a patch of the Dutch countryside near Lisse becomes one of the most photographed places on earth. Keukenhof Gardens covers 32 hectares of land planted with around seven million bulbs — over 800 varieties of tulips, plus hyacinths, daffodils, and narcissi — arranged in dense rivers of color between pavilions, ponds, and windmills. It has been doing this every year since 1950.

Tulips arrived in the Netherlands from the Ottoman Empire in the late 16th century and triggered "Tulip Mania" — arguably the world's first speculative financial bubble — in the 1630s. Today the Dutch bulb industry produces around three billion bulbs a year, most of which are exported. Keukenhof, created as a showcase for that industry, is where the results are put on display.

When to visit

Keukenhof opens for approximately eight weeks each spring, typically from late March to mid-May. Peak bloom for most tulip varieties falls in mid-April. The gardens plant bulbs in layers so that different varieties come into flower across the full season — early visitors see the first hyacinths and daffodils, late-season visitors see the last parrot and double tulips. Mid-April offers the broadest simultaneous range.

The outdoor fields: The most dramatic tulip images often come not from Keukenhof itself but from the open flower fields around Lisse, Hillegom, and Noordwijk nearby. These are commercial growing fields visible from the roads and cycle paths — a free, vast, and often less crowded alternative that complements the gardens perfectly.

The gardens in detail

Keukenhof is not a single garden but a collection of themed areas connected by paths. Different sections are designed by different Dutch bulb growers, each showcasing their own varieties in coordinated plantings. The result is an unusually dense horticultural display — seven million bulbs are planted in layers each autumn, meaning the color is continuous from ground to waist height in peak weeks.

Pavilions scattered through the gardens house cut-flower displays, orchid shows, and educational exhibitions about Dutch bulb cultivation. A windmill on the grounds is open to climb for elevated views over the surrounding fields.

Practical tips

Tickets

Tickets must be booked in advance at keukenhof.nl. The gardens operate a timed-entry system during peak weeks. Admission includes entry to all pavilions. Arrive early — before 10:00 AM — to avoid the heaviest crowds, which build through the late morning.

Getting there from Amsterdam

Direct bus connections run from Amsterdam Centraal and Schiphol Airport during the season. The journey takes around 35–45 minutes. Rental bikes and guided cycling tours through the bulb fields are available from Lisse — cycling through flower fields on a Dutch bicycle is arguably the classic Keukenhof experience.

What to wear and bring

Dutch spring weather is unpredictable — sunshine and rain can alternate within hours. Waterproof shoes are strongly recommended, as the paths become muddy after rain. The gardens are very large; comfortable walking footwear matters for a full visit, which typically takes 2–4 hours.
