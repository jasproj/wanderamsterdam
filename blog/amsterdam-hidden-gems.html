15 Hidden Gems in Amsterdam Locals Actually Go To

WanderAmsterdam editorial · Published 2026-08-30

Amsterdam's top attractions — the Rijksmuseum, Van Gogh, Anne Frank House — deserve their reputations. But the city's best moments often happen in the places most visitors walk right past: a hidden courtyard behind an unmarked door, a neighborhood market where no one speaks English, a canal that's prettier than the famous ones because no tour boats use it.

These are 15 places that locals actually go to, that most 3-day itineraries skip.

1. The Hofjes (Hidden Courtyards)

Medieval almshouse courtyards behind unmarked doors

Amsterdam's hofjes are enclosed courtyards originally built as housing for elderly women by wealthy benefactors. Many survive in the Jordaan and the city centre, hidden behind ordinary-looking doors in walls. The Karthuizerhofje (Karthuizersstraat 21–131), Claes Claeszhofje (Eerste Egelantiersdwarsstraat 3), and Sint Andrieshofje (Egelantiersgracht 107) are all visitable during daytime. Push the door gently — if it opens, you're welcome. Keep quiet; people live here.

Free · Jordaan

2. Brouwersgracht

The prettiest canal in Amsterdam — and the least crowded

Ask Amsterdammers which canal is their favorite and most say Brouwersgracht. The former brewery canal at the northern edge of the Jordaan has wider water, bigger houseboats, and a westward view that catches the sunset. Walk it from the Singel end toward Haarlemmerplein. Almost no tour boats pass through — it's a residential canal that happens to be gorgeous.

Free

3. Amsterdam Oost and the Dappermarkt

The most underrated neighborhood in the city

Amsterdam Oost (East) has the diversity that De Pijp is famous for but without the tourist overlay. Dappermarkt — a daily street market — is cheaper and more authentically multicultural than Albert Cuyp. Javastraat has Turkish bakeries, Surinamese takeaways, and trendy coffee shops side by side. The Tropenmuseum (tropical cultures) is one of Amsterdam's best and least visited museums. Oosterpark is the local Vondelpark without the crowds.

Tram 3 or 14 · Dappermarkt Mon–Sat

4. Marineterrein

Former naval base turned urban experiment

This former Dutch Navy compound next to Centraal Station opened to the public in 2015 and is still largely unknown to tourists. A floating swimming pool in the IJ harbour (free in summer), co-working spaces, experimental restaurants, and an urban beach. It feels like stumbling into a secret part of the city that hasn't been branded yet. Walk through from Kattenburgerstraat.

Free · 10 min walk from Centraal

5. Westerpark and the Westergasfabriek

Culture complex in a former gas factory

The Westergasfabriek is a cluster of repurposed industrial buildings in Westerpark — cinema, restaurants, a microbrewery (Brouwerij Troost), a Sunday market, and event spaces. The park itself is popular with locals for jogging and picnics but rarely appears on tourist maps. The Sunday Market (first Sunday of the month) has vintage, design, and food stalls.

Free to explore · Bus 21 or walk

6. Cycling to Waterland

Dutch countryside 15 minutes from Centraal Station

Take the free ferry to Noord, then cycle north toward Durgerdam, Ransdorp, and Holysloot. Within 15 minutes you're in open polder landscape — windmills, dykes, cows, and wooden houses reflected in still water. The loop through Waterland takes 2–3 hours by bike and is completely flat. Bring a picnic. This is the Netherlands that postcards promise and the city center can't show you.

Free · 2–3 hours by bike

7. De Hallen

A tram depot turned cultural hub

The former tram repair depot in Oud-West houses the Filmhallen (arthouse cinema), the Foodhallen (indoor food court), a public library, local shops, and a hotel. It's where locals in Oud-West go on rainy Saturday afternoons. The Local Goods Store sells only Amsterdam-made products. Less polished than the tourist center, more representative of how the city actually lives.

Oud-West · Tram 7 or 17

8. Amstelpark

A park with a working windmill that nobody visits

South of De Pijp, Amstelpark was built for the 1972 Floriade garden festival. It has a miniature golf course, a petting zoo, a rose garden, a hedge maze, a gallery in a glass house, and the Rieker windmill — a working 17th-century windmill on the Amstel River where Rembrandt used to sketch. Almost no tourists come here. Locals bring kids on weekends.

Free · South Amsterdam

9. Noordermarkt on Monday

The flea market version of Saturday's food market

Everyone knows the Saturday Noordermarkt (organic produce). The Monday version — a textile and antique market on the same square — is smaller, cheaper, and almost entirely local. Vintage clothing, secondhand books, old maps, and Dutch ceramics. Start at the market, then walk through the Jordaan's side streets. The Jordaan on a Monday morning is Amsterdam at its quietest and most charming.

Mondays 9am–1pm · Jordaan

10. Het Schip (Amsterdam School Architecture)

A housing block that's actually a masterpiece

Het Schip (The Ship) in the Spaarndammerbuurt neighborhood is the finest example of Amsterdam School architecture — an expressionist housing block built in 1919 for working-class families. The museum inside shows an original apartment and a former post office. The building's curved brickwork, decorative towers, and sculptural doorways are extraordinary. Architecture fans travel from around the world; most Amsterdam visitors have never heard of it.

~€10 · Bus 48 from Centraal

11. Flevopark and the Distillery

A park with its own jenever distillery

Flevopark in Oost has a public swimming lake (Flevoparkbad), walking paths, and — hidden in the park — Distilleerderij 't Nieuwe Diep, a craft distillery and café in an old water pumping station. Taste jenever, bitters, and liqueurs in a setting that feels like a countryside estate despite being a 15-minute tram ride from the centre.

Free park · Tram 14 to Flevopark

12. The Free Ferry to NDSM

The best free ride in Amsterdam

The ferry from behind Centraal Station to NDSM Wharf takes 15 minutes and crosses the full width of the IJ harbour. The view back toward the city — Centraal Station, the skyline, the EYE Film Museum — is the best free panorama in Amsterdam. At NDSM: street art, a weekend flea market, IJ-Hallen (Europe's largest flea market, monthly), and café-restaurants in old shipping containers.

Free · Runs frequently

13. Hortus Botanicus

One of the oldest botanical gardens in the world

Founded in 1638 as a medicinal herb garden for the city's doctors and pharmacists, the Hortus is now a peaceful escape in the Plantage neighborhood. The palm house, the butterfly greenhouse, and the three-climate glasshouse are the highlights. The coffee tree here is said to be the ancestor of all coffee plants in South and Central America — the Dutch East India Company smuggled it out of Yemen in the 1700s.

~€12.50 · Allow 1 hour

14. Prinseneiland

A secret island in the Western Islands

Three man-made islands — Prinseneiland, Bickerseiland, and Realeneiland — sit just west of Centraal Station. Originally warehouse districts for the Dutch East India Company, they're now quiet residential streets with canal-house conversions, drawbridges, and almost no foot traffic. Café de Vergulde Gaper on Prinseneiland is one of the best brown cafés in the city. Walk all three islands in an hour.

Free · 10 min walk from Centraal

15. Buikslotermeerplein Skatepark

The creative energy of Noord, concentrated

The area around Buikslotermeerplein in Noord — a short walk from the NDSM ferry — is where Amsterdam's youth culture actually happens. A free outdoor skatepark, street art walls, and the Tolhuistuin (a garden café and music venue in a former Shell canteen). It's raw, loud, and the opposite of a canal-ring tourist experience. The Tolhuistuin terrace on a summer evening is one of the best spots in the city.

Free · Ferry + 10 min walk
