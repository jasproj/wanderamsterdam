Utrecht & the Dom Tower: Netherlands' Tallest Church Tower

WanderAmsterdam editorial · Published 2026-10-22

Standing in the center of Utrecht, the Dom Tower rises 112.5 metres above the old city — the tallest church tower in the Netherlands. Building began in 1321 and finished in 1382, and the tower has dominated the Utrecht skyline ever since. In 1674, a violent storm destroyed the nave connecting the tower to the cathedral body; the tower has stood alone as a freestanding structure ever since, with an open square where the nave once was.

Utrecht itself is the Netherlands' fourth-largest city, a university town of around 360,000 people with a medieval center that survived the 20th century largely intact. Its Oudegracht (Old Canal) is lined at street level and at water level — a double-decker canal unique in the Netherlands, where medieval warehouse cellars open directly onto the water below the street, now occupied by cafés, restaurants, and galleries.

Climbing the Dom Tower

Guided tours of the Dom Tower run throughout the day. The climb covers over 465 steps through three stages, with a rest at each intermediate level. From the top, on a clear day, you can see Amsterdam to the north and Rotterdam to the south — the flat Dutch landscape stretching to the horizon in every direction.

The tower contains a carillon of 13 bells, the heaviest of which weighs around 8,200 kilograms. The carillon is played regularly by Utrecht's city carillonneur, and the sound carries across the entire old center on performance days.

Booking the climb: Tower entry requires a guided tour and advance booking is recommended, especially in summer. The climb is not suitable for those with vertigo or limited mobility. Children under 7 are generally not admitted to the upper sections.

The Oudegracht

Utrecht's most distinctive feature is its split-level canal. The Oudegracht was dug in the Middle Ages, with the street running along the top of the canal banks and the canal bottom several meters lower — low enough to allow merchant boats to unload directly into the ground-floor warehouses below street level. Those warehouse spaces (werfkelders) now house a long strip of restaurants and bars accessible from both the street above and the canal path below. Sitting at a canal-level terrace, with the water at your feet and the medieval buildings rising overhead, is one of the more atmospheric experiences in the Netherlands.

The Dom Cathedral and the square

The Domkerk (Dom Cathedral) still stands east of the tower, separated by the open square where the nave collapsed. The interior is Gothic, austere, and largely empty in the way of many former Catholic churches that became Protestant after the Reformation — which gives the space a particular quiet gravity.

Getting there from Amsterdam

Intercity trains from Amsterdam Centraal to Utrecht Centraal run every 15 minutes; the journey takes around 30 minutes. The Dom Tower is a 15-minute walk from Utrecht Centraal. Allow a full day to see the tower, walk the Oudegracht, and visit the Cathedral Museum. Guided walking tours of Utrecht are available through Amsterdam day trip operators.
