Haarlem: The Art City 15 Minutes from Amsterdam

WanderAmsterdam editorial · Published 2026-10-22

Haarlem is 20 kilometers west of Amsterdam and about 15 minutes by train — close enough to visit in a half day and different enough to justify the trip. It is one of the Netherlands' most complete medieval city centers, with a spectacular main square (the Grote Markt) that still looks roughly as it did four centuries ago, a Gothic cathedral that took 150 years to build, and a museum dedicated to one of the giants of Dutch Golden Age painting.

During the 17th century, Haarlem rivaled Amsterdam as a center of Dutch art. Frans Hals worked here his entire career; so did Jacob van Ruisdael, Adriaen van Ostade, and other painters who collectively shaped what we now call the Dutch Golden Age style. Amsterdam's own art scene owed a significant debt to Haarlem, which developed the conventions of realistic portraiture, landscape painting, and genre scenes that Rembrandt and Vermeer would later perfect.

The Frans Hals Museum

The Frans Hals Museum occupies two locations in Haarlem, both worth visiting. The Hof location (Groot Heiligland 62) is housed in a former almshouse from 1610 — a beautiful courtyard building that is itself a historic monument. The collection here focuses on Hals and his contemporaries, including his famous large group portraits of militia companies and regents that pre-dated Rembrandt's Night Watch by several decades and established the genre.

Frans Hals is sometimes underrated beside Rembrandt, but his best works — the laughing portraits, the loose brushwork that anticipates Impressionism by two centuries — are genuinely extraordinary. The museum is rarely crowded, and the space allows for close examination of large canvases that would be jostled in a busier venue.

The Hal location: The second Frans Hals Museum building (Grote Markt 16) is the former Vleeshal (Meat Hall) on the main square. It hosts temporary exhibitions of contemporary art. Both locations are covered by a single admission ticket.

The Grote Kerk and the main square

The Church of St Bavo (Grote Kerk) dominates the Grote Markt and the entire city skyline. Construction began in the 14th century and continued for roughly 150 years. The interior is a single enormous Gothic space — one of the most impressive church interiors in the Netherlands — with a famous 18th-century Christian Müller organ that Mozart played as a child on a visit to Haarlem and that Handel also played. The organ remains in use for regular concerts.

The Grote Markt is an unusually intact medieval market square. The former meat hall (Vleeshal), the fish market (Vishal), and the historic town hall all face the church across the cobblestones. On Saturdays a regular market fills the square.

Getting there and around

Trains from Amsterdam Centraal to Haarlem run every 10–15 minutes; the journey takes around 15 minutes. The city center is a 10-minute walk from Haarlem station. Most of the key sights are within easy walking distance of each other. Allow at least half a day; a full day if you want to visit both Frans Hals Museum locations and walk the canal streets near the Spaarne river.
